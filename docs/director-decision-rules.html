Director decision rules

Status: Governing for the 2026-09-13 delivery and for Director coordination after it. Adopted by owner direction on 2026-09-08.

These are decision procedures, not aspirations. Each one states what it changes about the work, and each is written so that a lane can apply it without asking the Director. They remove no deterministic gate, relax no approval, and create no authority. Where a rule and AGENTS.md or src/director_orchestrator/policy.py disagree, the gate wins.

R1 — Sequence checks by failure probability per unit cost

Rule. When several conditions must all hold, determine them in decreasing order of p_fail / cost_to_determine. Check the thing most likely to fail and cheapest to check, first.

order by:  p(this condition is unmet) / (time to find out)     descending

Why it binds here. The activation chain is roughly a dozen sequential gates, each able to refuse. If they were independent at 90% each, a clean first pass has probability 0.9^12 ≈ 0.28. Expect a refusal; the only real question is how early you learn about it.

Distinguish two costs. The cost of determining whether a gate is satisfied is usually far below the cost of satisfying it. Gate 8 is the clearest case: the Long Table canary bundle either exists or it does not, and looking takes minutes, while producing it takes days.

What this changes. Gate 8 is determined on Wednesday, not at the Saturday go/no-go. Every gate whose determination is cheap is determined before any gate whose satisfaction is expensive. The Saturday go/no-go becomes a confirmation of a mostly-known answer rather than the moment of discovery.

Who applies it. Director, when ordering any checklist; any lane, when choosing which of several unknowns to resolve first.

R2 — The gate ledger is three-valued and computed, never asserted

Rule. Every gate carries a value in {true, false, unknown}. Completion is computed by Kleene conjunction: false if any gate is false; unknown if any gate is unknown and none is false; true only when every gate is true.

Done      = AND over gates                    ( {T,F,U} )
Permitted = Done  AND  NOT (OR over no-go conditions)

Both must be evaluated. A satisfied conjunction never overrides a triggered no-go.

Why it binds here. The failure this project is exposed to is not a gate reading false — it is a gate reading unknown and being counted as true. This is the formal statement of the rule already in AGENTS.md: absence of evidence is never consent, and a provider that is out of quota, unauthenticated, silent or absent has not approved anything.

What this changes. The ledger in the activation plan stores T, F or U per gate with the evidence reference beside it, and the completion line is derived from those values. No document, status message or report states that the project is done; each states the computed value and the gates still unknown.

Who applies it. Director, in every status and in the close-out report. Any lane reporting a gate reports one of the three values and never omits U.

R3 — An optional action must name the decision it could flip

Rule. Before any optional investigation, status check, review pass, or provider call, name at least one decision whose outcome could change as a result. If no possible result changes any action, the information is worth nothing regardless of how uncertain the question feels, and the action is not taken.

value of information = 0   whenever every outcome leads to the same next action
paid probe worthwhile iff  p(decisive) × (time or cost saved) > cost of probe

Why it binds here. A repeat of an unchanged attempt has p(decisive) ≈ 0 by construction, which is why blind retries are already forbidden. The rule generalises that prohibition from retries to all optional work.

What this changes. Every work order that proposes an investigation states the decision it serves. The Director does not re-read settled files, and does not re-run a check whose result cannot move a gate. Required security, CI, approval and exact-candidate checks are mandatory and out of scope for this rule — optionality is what the rule tests, not importance.

Who applies it. Every lane, before spending a paid attempt or an investigation pass.

R4 — The schedule is a temporal network, not a critical path

Rule. Model the schedule as constraints of both signs and check consistency, rather than summing a longest path.

minimum lag:   t_j − t_i ≥ d_ij      precedence
maximum lag:   t_j − t_i ≤ D_ij      expiry, deadline, freshness window
release:       t_i ≥ r_i             an owner window opens
capacity:      attended tasks do not overlap        (operator capacity 1)

feasible  iff  the constraint graph contains no negative cycle

Why it binds here. Two constraints in this delivery are maximum lags, which critical-path analysis cannot express. LIVE_OK evidence is valid for at most 86,400 seconds with no renewal path, so t(Activate) − t(finish ValidateProviders) ≤ 24 h. And the owner windows are release times on a resource of capacity one.

A consequence that ordinary scheduling intuition gets backwards. Because the freshness window cannot be renewed and the evidence campaign spends budget, ValidateProviders should start as late as the buffer allows, not as early as possible. Earliness is a cost here.

What this changes. WO-14 runs the consistency check before Thursday's window. If it reports a negative cycle, the plan is infeasible as written and the specific constraint pair is named, which is a far more useful failure than discovering the conflict on Saturday.

Who applies it. Director, before committing any window.

R5 — Operator-minutes is the binding resource

Rule. Every task that requires the owner present carries a duration estimate. The feasibility test is:

Σ attended durations  ≤  Σ available owner-window minutes

with capacity one. No amount of delegation, parallelism or additional providers changes either side of that inequality.

Why it binds here. Every control action requires the operator for its whole pass, and the writer lease is single-writer by design. The stated windows total roughly fourteen and a half hours. That number, not compute, decides whether the plan fits.

What this changes. WO-14 sums the attended estimates against the windows. If the sum exceeds the budget, scope moves — the response is never "add an agent."

Corollary on parallelism. Split the project in two before reasoning about concurrency. The engineering lane has a high parallel fraction; the activation lane has essentially none, and concurrent writers there are an explicit no-go. For the engineering lane, useful concurrency is bounded by the number of disjoint file partitions with independent tests — a number that can be counted off a diff rather than estimated:

Speedup(N) ≈ 1 / [ (1−P) + P/N + c·N(N−1)/2 ]      interior maximum near N* ≈ sqrt(2P/c)

Who applies it. Director, when sizing a window; owner, when accepting one.

R6 — Evidence carries a binding key and is reused on match

Rule. Every piece of verification carries an explicit key, and is reusable exactly when the key still matches.

K(e) = ( artifact identity, inputs, environment, issuer, freshness )

work remaining = Σ over required checks r of  cost(r) · [ no valid evidence satisfies K(r) ]

Key granularity is the whole lever. Coarse keys invalidate everything on every change; fine keys survive. The repository already computes tree digests, so a per-subtree key is available at no cost.

The line that never moves. The exact-candidate review verdict, CI, and security results stay bound to the candidate commit. That is a security property, not an inefficiency, and a changed head always needs its own verdicts. Historical approvals never transfer. The reuse applies to supporting analysis — a blocker audit keyed to specific files, a topology answer, a control-script behaviour question — which today would be discarded on a rebase that does not touch those files.

What this changes. WO-02, WO-03 and WO-05 record each finding with the file paths and digests it depends on, so a candidate change re-runs only the findings whose inputs moved.

Who applies it. Every lane producing analysis; the Director when reconciling evidence.

R7 — Allocation quotas are integers

Rule. Over a rolling window W, convert target shares to integer quotas by largest-remainder apportionment so the quotas sum to W exactly, and express the bands and ceilings as integer bounds.

q_i = round to integers by largest remainder,  Σ q_i = W
floor((s_i − 0.03)·W)  ≤  n_i  ≤  floor((s_i + 0.05)·W)

At W = 20, a 40% target is 8 assignments with a ceiling of 9. A zero target refuses a lane in that profile, including for accepted non-code research; the same lane is allocation-admissible under a profile with a non-zero target, subject to remaining capacity and other admission gates. In particular, engineering refuses Perplexity and Firecrawl research (both targets 0%), while public_research allows the same requests through allocation admission with targets of 25% and 15% respectively, when the other conditions pass.

is_accepted_productive_kind, workload_policy.py:512–529 classifies these lanes' research as productive. The evaluate_assignment admission call site, workload_policy.py:993–1002, applies that classification before the capacity check; the zero-target check, lines 617–618, returns allocation_profile_lane_ineligible. allocation_profiles.py:17–18 defines the two target sets. Admission and concurrency rules are unchanged; this wording grants no dispatch authority and activates no profile.

What these rules do not do

They do not reduce the thirty provider-activation evidence rows, the nine activation gates, or the attended minutes those require. Those are fixed by the system's design and by its safety properties. What the rules change is the order in which unknowns are resolved, the amount of work that is repeated unnecessarily, and whether an unknown can be mistaken for a pass.

No percentage reduction is claimed. The largest risk to this delivery is not scheduling but whatever the blocker audit surfaces that nobody has looked at yet, and no decision rule surfaces that.
