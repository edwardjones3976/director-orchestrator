Foreman-controlled repair work orders

One responsible Foreman, one explicit procedure, and evidence reused where it still proves the requirement. This procedure covers Director-Orchestrator repairs, not ordinary assignments or other projects.

Authority · Work order · Research · Five phases · Evidence reuse · Reporting · Recommendations · Rollout

Authority and operating boundary

All Director-Orchestrator repairs are routed to the Foreman. Only the acting Foreman issues mandatory, direct repair instructions. Once issued, providers follow the prescribed solution, sequence and boundaries. They may recommend alternatives or skipped steps, but must receive a revised Foreman order before acting on those recommendations. Outside Foreman-directed repairs, administrators and providers retain judgment and problem-solving freedom within their assignments.

A repair corrects an observed defect or inconsistency in project code, documentation, CI, provider plumbing or operations. New features and ordinary delivery assignments continue under their existing outcome-based guidance. An investigation finding is evidence, not permission to implement a fix. If classification or instructions conflict, pause only the affected step and return the conflict to the Foreman.

Issue one complete, bounded order

Use the repair work-order template and one maintained repair report. Ordinary work keeps the provider assignment template. Templates are drafts, not execution authority. The Foreman fills every execution field and marks the order ISSUED before provider execution. No unresolved placeholder may remain for a command, path, limit, owner, contact, deadline or acceptance condition. A reasoned “not applicable” is valid; UNKNOWN is not an executable instruction.

An unknown cause receives an investigation-only order first. It authorizes named read-only checks and a report, not a speculative fix. If an isolated reproduction requires a write or mutation, that action needs an explicit investigation amendment before use. Once the cause is established, the Foreman issues a repair-stage revision with the selected implementation instructions. Do not conceal an authorization gap under “use best judgment.”

Required Foreman research

Every Foreman repair case requires relevant Firecrawl or Perplexity research. Normally perform it when the repair is requested, before selecting the repair procedure. The Foreman may authorize bounded initial checks first, but the issued order must name the research step, route, owner and checkpoint that completes before any repair limit is reached, including its attempt, revision, hop, time or spending ceiling. Do not consume the final allowance while research is pending. A new order ID, handoff or limit amendment does not erase the case's research obligation.

Retain sanitized queries, source URLs, original tool/operation receipts, actual observations, relevance to the defect and proposed solution, and measured usage or UNKNOWN. Prefer relevant primary sources; distinguish their external context from private project evidence and hypotheses. Keep credentials, private logs and administrator matters out of external queries. Applicable prior Firecrawl/Perplexity research may be reused through a recorded Foreman decision binding its receipt, coverage and current inputs. Reuse is completed research, not an exemption or an invented receipt.

If the route fails, is ambiguous or would exceed authority/budget, report the obstacle and hold dependent repair work before its limit. Reconcile uncertain effects before retry. Research does not expand permissions, waive independent review or create spending authority. This is operating guidance; existing runtime contracts remain unchanged.

Five execution phases

  1. Intake and evidence assessment. Read the existing report and receipts. Reconcile current source/configuration, active writer, review claim, task state and external operation IDs. Check the register and merged PRs. Perform or bind applicable Firecrawl/Perplexity research, or record its bounded mandatory checkpoint before case limits. Do not reopen a merged repair because an old checklist remains unchecked. Return stale or contradictory facts before dependent work.
  2. Investigation, when required. Inspect only the named logs, configurations and producer/consumer paths. Compare expected and observed behavior. Establish the prescribed minimal reproduction and distinguish a supported root cause from hypotheses. Preserve contradictions, negative results and uncertainty. Submit findings before requesting a repair-stage order.
  3. Repair authorization and execution. Acknowledge the issued revision before the first mutation. Follow the Foreman's selected solution and numbered sequence; change only named surfaces. Unexpected source drift, side effects, required files or conditions pause the affected step. Recommend an amendment with evidence and wait for the revised order; disjoint authorized work may continue. At prescribed step 3.2, capture actual resulting head_sha, tree_sha, current target-tip base_sha, review merge_base_sha and applicable configuration digests after approved edits/commit. Before issue, specify exact capture commands, working directory, output destination and failure branch. The Foreman confirms outputs in a dated candidate-binding appendix or issued revision within cumulative limits, and links a newly digested immutable candidate packet for v1 work. An evidenced grandfathered plan keeps its original-plan binding and limits; do not force a v1 transition. Do not predict a future SHA. Prescribed provisional working-tree tests may run; final-candidate validation and independent review wait for this checkpoint.
  4. Validation and independent evaluation. Confirm the bound resulting bytes and run or explicitly reuse named regression and failure controls against the real producer-to-consumer path. Where prescribed, show pre-fix failure in a separate controlled fixture/worktree. For a GitHub-backed handoff, prescribe a parseable input: the native Actions pull_request/push envelope, or a local JSON object {"pull_request": <unaltered actual REST PR object>}. Preserve the original API response separately and identify this local wrapper; a raw Pulls API response is not direct checker input. Verify its repository, source head, target ref/base and provenance against the frozen candidate/current target tip. Run scripts/check_contribution_tags.py with exact target-tip base_sha, resulting head_sha, verified default-branch reference and --github-event pointing to that envelope. Retain origin, capture time, digest, command and exit/result. The checker can select the event head instead of --head; independently reject mismatches before invocation. Its structure/range result does not authenticate the event, prove repository/base consistency or establish authorship. Identify the API-derived wrapper separately from the native Actions envelope. Without an event, label a local check revision-only and hold required event evidence pending. Non-GitHub work records a reasoned not-applicable event decision. Freeze the confirmed candidate for eligible review and current CI/security/publication gates; changed candidates require current binding.
  5. Closeout. Submit the HTML report before handoff or a completion claim. Record actual outcome, limitations, cleanup and remaining owner. Distinguish implemented, tested, reviewed, merged, deployed and live-verified. A merge is not deployment; an acknowledgment is not a provider result. Update the existing queue, not a replacement assignment.

Evidence reuse and amendments

Providers may recommend an omitted or reused step by supplying its step ID, existing evidence location, source/configuration binding, coverage and why it remains applicable. The Foreman records approval or rejection, reason, revision and time before execution changes. Reused evidence must establish the same requirement; a persuasive narrative is insufficient.

Changed inputs invalidate the affected evidence, not unrelated completed work. Record the actual delta and affected consumers. Existing exact-candidate review and CI requirements still apply; reusable diagnostics do not make an old-head verdict current. Carry the same repair-case identity and cumulative spending, attempt, hop and revision counters across order revisions, new order IDs, provider handoffs and transport changes; none resets the inherited ceilings. Record consumed and remaining counts, and stop at exhaustion until the responsible authority explicitly amends a limit. Historical failures, cancelled runs and prior order revisions remain evidence. Amend the current order, preserve the superseded revision and append the change/decision history; do not restart the whole process by default.

One HTML report and existing receipts

Maintain one report per repair, linked from its existing issue/PR and register entry. Include findings and reproduction, root-cause assessment, executed instructions, approved deviations and omitted steps, actual files/configuration changes and exact identifiers, test commands and results (including skips and failures), risks, prevention recommendations, cleanup, follow-up owner, delivery, acknowledgment and final disposition. Escape untrusted log text before embedding it in HTML.

Tool and resource ledger

Reference the original receipts rather than duplicating logs. Record actual provider/model (served identity or UNKNOWN), tool/version, host/runner, purpose, operation ID, start/end time, result, evidence location, measured tokens/credits/time or UNKNOWN, spend and unresolved holds, configuration changes, tool failures, maintenance performed and cleanup status/owner. Record equipment or software used even when it failed. Secret references identify the approved store or variable name, never credential contents. A local estimate is not measured spend. This is a table in the existing report/register, not new telemetry machinery.

Recommendations for the Foreman

The Foreman selects and turns these into exact instructions for the named candidate. They do not authorize providers to substitute a solution.

Integrate without duplicate work

  1. Reconcile the current queue with Claude using the existing staff workbook, repair register, issues and PR claims. Preserve user edits and append history. Record already completed repairs as completed; distinguish remaining acceptance/release work.
  2. Send this boundary through the established Claude, Cursor, Astra and affected-provider channels or their next relevant assignments. Record actual delivery and acknowledgment, or UNKNOWN. Do not wake every model just for acknowledgment.
  3. Issue an order only for verified remaining work with one writer, exact scope and resource limits. Use the first real repair as the workflow check; if none is ready, record the check as pending rather than invent a repair.
  4. Use existing documentation checks and scenario review below. Do not run a simulated campaign or repeat a full product suite to validate this operating procedure.
