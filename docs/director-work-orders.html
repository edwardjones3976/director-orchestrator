Director work orders and Orchestrator admission

The Director issues general work orders for major work to any eligible provider. The Foreman issues the narrower repair work order to workers. Before a major Director order is dispatched, the Orchestrator admits and sequences it so the workforce, resources and evidence path can be monitored as one workflow.

Two order classes

Authority follows the purpose of the order, not its informal name
OrderIssuerEligible assigneesUse

“All providers” means every provider is eligible for Director consideration when its verified capability and current authority fit; it does not require broadcasting every order or assigning every provider. Assign each responsibility once, keep one writer per surface and preserve independent review.

Major plans pass through the Orchestrator

  1. The Director defines the outcome, authority boundary, acceptance gates and owner-reserved decisions.
  2. The Orchestrator checks dependencies, active writers, provider/model/effort fit, workload, RAM and other shared resources, attempts, cost, deadlines, communication routes, review independence and observable checkpoints.
  3. The Orchestrator records ADMIT, ADMIT_WITH_CONDITIONS or HOLD in the order or task receipt. A session holding both Director and Orchestrator roles records both decisions; it does not message itself or claim two identities.
  4. Only an admitted order is dispatched. The Orchestrator then monitors receipts, pending dependencies, partial results, timeouts, ambiguous effects and handoffs through the existing workflow.

Admission improves execution; it cannot rewrite the Director's objective, expand authority or waive a deterministic gate. A hold names the missing prerequisite and the responsible next action. A provider response, dispatch acknowledgment or generated file is not completion.

Minimum Director order

Use the Director work-order template or an equivalent native packet. Bind the purpose, exact scope and candidate, accountable owner, assignee and provider identity, permitted and prohibited actions, inputs, dependencies, resource and concurrency plan, attempt/time/token/cost ceilings, checkpoints, deliverables, tests, independent review, stop conditions, recovery, communication and evidence destinations. Record Orchestrator admission and the actual dispatch receipt separately.

Large work should be decomposed into dependency-ordered, independently observable assignments. Parallelize disjoint low-memory lanes; serialize conflicting writers and heavy local workloads. A changed transport or assignee does not reset attempts, cost, deadlines or unresolved effects.

Abnormal Foreman delegation

A Foreman repair order goes to a worker unless one of these conditions is evidenced: no eligible worker can access the required surface after bounded current checks; an active administrator writer must preserve continuity to avoid unsafe transfer; incident containment requires administrator-only controls; or every eligible worker route is unavailable and delaying the repair would create a material safety, integrity or recovery risk.

Before assigning an administrator, the Foreman records the abnormal condition, why worker delegation is unavailable or unsafe, the administrator's exact bounded action, Director/Orchestrator concurrence, inherited cumulative limits and a separate eligible reviewer. Convenience, speed, seniority, a preferred model or an unverified availability assumption is not abnormal. The exception expires with that repair and never transfers the Foreman appointment or permits self-review.

Rejection and return

A rejecting provider returns the original immutable reference and digest, findings, evidence, and a separately identified proposed revision to the original sender. Include the full original only when the sender cannot reliably retrieve the immutable bytes, the source is mutable or ephemeral, the handoff crosses an inaccessible system, or offline archival requires a self-contained packet. The proposal is advisory: the responsible writer decides what to adopt, changed bytes receive fresh tests and review, and the rejector cannot approve its own revision.

Secretary response priority

Antigravity Secretary performs administrative documentation and responds in this default order unless the owner explicitly changes it for a task: owner/user; Director; Orchestrator; Foreman; Warden and separately appointed Quality/Verification leads; other administrators; assigned workers. Within one rank, an explicit deadline, safety or integrity blocker, and then receipt time determines order.

The Secretary may acknowledge lower-priority requests and preserve their deadlines while higher-priority work is active, but it does not silently drop them. Conflicting directions are held and escalated to the highest-ranked available source. Priority changes response order only; it grants no decision, assignment, spending, credential, review, merge, deployment or self-approval authority.

Operating recommendations adopted with this policy

  • Use one task identity and one evidence ledger from Director plan through Orchestrator admission, provider execution, review and handoff.
  • Prefer references and digests over duplicate payloads; require self-contained returns only when retrieval cannot be trusted.
  • Measure pending work and terminal outcomes explicitly. Preserve full replies as they arrive and label deadline expirations partial.
  • Keep the local Director in standby or supporting mode for Foreman, Warden and Orchestrator continuity; activation never silently displaces the appointed role holder.
  • Review this taxonomy at the first real major order and first abnormal Foreman exception; documentation publication is not runtime adoption.
