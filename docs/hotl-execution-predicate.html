Standing-authorization execution predicate

This describes the executable form of the delegation rules. It adds nothing to them; the pre-execution class below is the governing document's own stated carve-out, not an addition made here. human-out-of-the-loop-operation.md remains authoritative. Where the two could ever disagree, the governing document wins and this one is wrong.

This integrates the evaluated predicate. The tier lists stay closed; their one owner-signed addition, execute_small_read_only_task, is decided by decide_pre_execution, described below, and never by H(a). The current owner protocol requires one non-author reviewer, who carries the merge. Tests pin a local copy of the tier authority so checkout depth and remote names cannot silently disable the gate comparison.

The predicate

A proposed candidate-bound action runs without returning to the owner only when every term holds (the one pre-execution class is decided instead by decide_pre_execution, under The pre-execution class):

H(a) = A(a) and G(a) and B(a) and V(a) and I(a) and S(a) and P(a) and D(a)

The right-hand column names the numbered item of the governing document's evidence bar that each term carries. That mapping is the load-bearing part: a bar item reaching no term is not a relaxed requirement, it is an unevaluated one, and the predicate returns execute without ever reading it.

Term Required condition Bar
A authorization A grant is live, unexpired, unrevoked, and scoped to this repository, branch, decision class and assignment. 9
G gates Every required CI check is green at the exact candidate head, independent security evidence exists at that same head, and no tier-0 class is implicated directly or as a consequence. 1, 2, 7
B bounds Budget, attempt and hop ledgers are all within their configured bounds. 8
V verification Evidence is current and bound to the exact candidate; any earlier ambiguous attempt is reconciled. —
I independence One exact-SHA ACCEPT from a non-author reviewer; author and reviewer are distinct identities, and that reviewer performs the merge. 3, 4, 5
S safe stopping Failure handling, cancellation and any required recovery are defined and authorized. —
P path scope The change touches only paths inside the assignment's owned_paths and none of its prohibited_paths. 6
D durable audit The audit record for the decision is durably written before the decision executes. 10

P and D are recent. The predicate was first implemented as the six-letter form from the owner's approved analysis, taken literally, and that form is short by two: items 6 and 10 mapped to nothing, so a change reaching prohibited paths with no audit record satisfied H(a). An independent review of #146 found it. Adding the two terms adds approval requirements, which AGENTS.md rule 4 permits; removing one is what it forbids.

I follows the owner's September 12 single-reviewer protocol. It does not require a second verdict or a third executing identity.

It is a conjunction, not a score. One unmet term is decisive, and test_any_single_unmet_term_blocks exercises that for each of the eight. A term this predicate does not recognise is refused rather than ignored, because silently dropping one is exactly how items 6 and 10 went missing.

The tier check runs first

classify() is evaluated before any term, and fails closed. Tier 1 is the closed four-class list in the governing document; anything not literally in it is tier 0 and can never run autonomously, however many terms pass. A grant that names an unlisted class does not create one.

This ordering is the load-bearing part. Evaluating the conjunction first and the tier afterwards would let eight satisfied terms carry a production deployment, which is why test_a_tier_zero_action_never_executes_even_with_every_term_met asserts the negative directly.

The pre-execution class

execute_small_read_only_task is tier 1, but H(a) never decides it. It is decided before any candidate or verdict exists, so G (items 1, 2, 7) and I (items 3, 4, 5) have nothing to read, and recording them as satisfied would be exactly the unevaluated-term failure above. decide() therefore holds it, and decide_pre_execution() decides it over its own closed list, PRE_EXECUTION_TERMS:

Term Required condition Bar
authorization A grant is live, unexpired, unrevoked, unexhausted and untripped, and covers this class, repository, branch and both lanes; the intake is an attested owner message. 9
eligibility policy.small_read_only_eligible() admits the task: no deterministic approval reason matches (a keyword scan), and the request is read-shaped. This is lexical, not evidence that no tier-0 class is implicated, so item 7 is not evaluated before execution. —
bounds Hop and revision limits are within their configured bounds. 8
verification The exact starting commit and snapshot digest are bound. —
lane_independence Two distinct lanes from different provider families. Structural only; it is not a verdict. —
safe_stopping A positive provider timeout is configured. —
owned_paths_declared Owned paths are declared and no write surface is granted, before any change exists. 6
audit The PRE_AUTHORIZED record is durably written before execution. 10

Items 1 to 5 are carried by the task's own exact_sha_review and ci_security_completion graph gates, which run unchanged. Item 7 is not evaluated before execution; whether this class's lexical eligibility is enough to replace the per-task button is part of the owner's sign-off. Every term is required, none recovers, and an unlisted or missing term is refused.

A hold is not a permission question

For every candidate-bound class (the pre-execution class uses decide_pre_execution, where every term is required and none recovers):

Decision(a) = execute        if H(a)
              recover        if EVERY unmet term authorizes its own recovery,
                             and none of them is owner-routed
              hold(blocker)  otherwise

That quantifier is the whole rule. "If an unmet term authorizes a recovery" is existential, and it lets one term's remedy carry every other unmet term: a red gates paired with a recoverable verification recovers, advancing a decision whose CI is failing on the strength of a re-verification that has nothing to do with it. The code was corrected first and this page was left behind, still specifying the rejected form — the same mistake, in the same shape, as the governing document keeping the six-term H(a). This page is the only place Decision(a) is written down, so leaving it would have restored the bug in the next implementation to read it.

Owner-routed terms are NON_RECOVERABLE_TERMS: authorization, gates, independence, scope and audit. A recovery string attached to any of them changes nothing, because each is a trip condition or an explicit refusal in the governing document.

A hold names the blocking term and what is missing. It does not re-ask the owner. Missing or stale evidence routes to verification; a temporary failure routes to its authorized recovery; only a blocker needing authority outside the standing grant reaches a person. test_a_blocker_is_never_phrased_as_a_ permission_question rejects blocker text containing a question mark or asking language, because a system that renames an interruption has not removed one.

Only the first available recovery is offered. Recoveries re-enter this function from the top, so chaining them internally would skip a fresh reading of every term.

Measuring it

autonomous completion rate =
    eligible workflows completed correctly within bounds, without new human
    intervention
    ------------------------------------------------------------------
    all eligible workflows whose deadline has passed

Eligibility and deadline are fixed before execution, so blocked or failed work cannot leave the denominator by later reclassification. An empty population reports no rate rather than 100 percent, which test_an_empty_population_has_no_rate_rather_than_a_flattering_one pins.

The provider-evidence expiry window, measured

There is no expiry-window constant. expires_at is supplied per record, and the only shape rules are that it is UTC, later than observed_at, and in the future when recorded.

The operational constraint is stronger than a deadline on activation. _surface_readiness scans every record in the five-stage chain and reports the surface not ready if any one has expired:

for record in records:
    if record.expires_at <= now:
        return ActivationSurfaceReadiness(surface, None, False, False,
                                          f"{record.stage.value} evidence expired", ...)

Two consequences follow, and both differ from the intuitive reading:

  • LIVE_OK does not settle the question. The earliest record, INSTALLED, keeps the surface live only while it is unexpired. When it lapses, live_ready goes false and live dispatch stops, however recent LIVE_OK is. The condition is continuing, not a race to activation.
  • Letting the prior stage lapse mid-chain does not merely cost a retry: advancing then raises the prior activation stage expired; use a new binding version, discarding the whole chain for that binding.

So advances belong immediately before activation, and the expiry chosen at advance time must cover the intended operating life of the lane, not just the gap to Activate.
